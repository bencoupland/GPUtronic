# Wrap-up, 12 October 2026

Notes only. No code changed and no tests run for this note.

## Tonight's conclusions

1. **The 29% spread is two flat plateaus.** In the 30-second open-loop hold at 46 SMs, blocks 0 to 35 ran at about 452k ops per host bin and blocks 36 to 45 at about 829k, 1.83 times faster. Each group varies only a few percent inside. The ranking never moved over the 30 seconds. Detail: `docs/analysis/load-imbalance-2026-10-12.md` on `test-results/2026-10-12_load-imbalance` (commit `fa1aa9b`).
2. **Grok Build's theory.** The 5080 is 7 groups of 12 SMs. Blocks 0 to 35 fill three groups, and the 10 fast blocks partly fill a fourth. The step is the boundary of that partly filled group. Caveat: 12 SMs per group is an inference. NVIDIA only states 7 GPCs and 84 SMs for GB203 and does not give the per-GPC split.
3. **Block-to-SM placement is still unverified.** The tally slot is `blockIdx.x`, not the physical SM. CUDA does not promise one block per SM in index order.
4. **Archive finding from 23 September** (`TRANSFER.md` on `archive/lab-2026-10-05`). Working SMs speed up as others drop out: Z 1.49 at 48 SMs against the 1.75 equal shares would give. That points to a shared bottleneck such as the mapped atomic, L2, or power. It fits the fast blocks being the ones with idle neighbours.

## Next steps, in order

1. **Record each block's physical SM ID at launch.** This touches the slot write in `src/gputronic_gold.cu` (about line 144 and line 221), the host CSV writer near line 1600, and the per-SM keying in `scripts/analyze_smaccum.py`.
2. **Rerun the open-loop tally at 36, 46, and 48 SMs.** If 36 and 48 are flat and 46 still steps, the partly filled group is the cause.
3. **One 46-SM hold with idle blocks not reading the mapped words**, to separate idle polling from the group boundary.
4. **Do not retune.** Keep Kp 0.55, Ki 0.08, deadband 0.08, and the scaled integral clamp. The closed loop sits on the 46-to-48 edge, which is a reason to measure that edge, not a reason to change gains.
