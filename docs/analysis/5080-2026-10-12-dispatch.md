## Keep the table and the two-sided law

The one-sided law on this driver, the 4 October binary, still holds Z 1.34 at about
49 SMs. The new law holds Gold near 1.49, transfer at 1.46, and episode near 1.47,
with the same gains. The starting count now comes from the rate table, and the PI

The episode dyno is why the half-width probe was the wrong ruler. At 42 SMs, Z is
about 1.19. At 21 SMs, Z is about 1.63. The table puts `n_ff` near 27. The closed
loop then sits around 23 to 25.

## The 0.015 deadband is not what bought the tenth

One more Gold run, after the suite. Tensor sensor off. `GPUTRONIC_DEADBAND=0.08`,
which is the old band. Default in the source is still 0.015. The environment
variable is off unless it is set. Thirty seconds, target 1.5, Kp 0.55, Ki 0.08.

The table on that run: Z at 63 SMs was 1.189, Z at 42 was 1.633, `n_ff` 48.3,
`sms_per_u` 47.4, full-width reference 589k pulses/s.

Last 20 seconds:

| | Deadband 0.015, tensor off (T2) | Deadband 0.08, tensor off |
|---|---|---|
| Mean Z | 1.471 | 1.516 |
| Z standard deviation | 0.069 | 0.038 |
| SM count | mean 53, range 50–58 | 46 on 94% of samples, range 45–51 |
| Integral | mean 0.58 | mean 0.001, frozen |
| Pulse rate | 389k | 389k |

The 1 Hz print sat on 46 SMs from t = 4 s through t = 30 s. Ninety-eight percent of
the samples were inside |Z − 1.50| < 0.08, so the law held the count and did not
integrate. The two brief steps off 46 are the samples that left the band.

This is one probe, not a sweep. It landed well: `n_ff` 48, and the first second
after the table was Z 1.41, just outside the band, so the law cut two SMs and then
froze. A probe that lands at Z 1.35 would still be outside 0.08 and would still
trim. The wide band does not freeze a bad start. It freezes a start that is already
inside 1.42 to 1.58.

The 0.015 band is smaller than the Z movement, which is about 0.05 to 0.07, so the
count never rests. The mean is fine either way. The width left for other work is
quiet only with the wider band. I would put the deadband back near 0.08 on the
two-sided law and keep the table. I did not change the default. The run is
`logs/2026-10-12_5080/T2_deadband08.txt` and `traces_10ms/T2_deadband08.csv`.

## Episode and the clamp

The integral is clamped at 1. Ki times that clamp times `sms_per_u` is about four
SMs. The proportional term adds a couple more while the error is outside the band.
A low `n_ff` cannot be walked far.

The first episode closed loop hit the clamp and sat near Z 1.43 at about 20 SMs.
The re-run, after the free-run sample was moved, did not. From t = 8 s, mean Z
1.467, count mean 24 (range 21–28), integral mean 0.55 and maximum 0.83. The clamp
is what happens when the table undershoots. It was not the steady state of the
second run.

## Leave the tensor warp off

At 84 SMs it cuts the CUDA-core rate from 555k to 337k pulses/s. The tensor counter
then runs at half that rate at every width, so the two Z values are the same number.
The selector has nothing to choose. With the warp off, the full-width rate matches
the old law's ceiling on this driver. I would not ship the sensor on until a kernel
exists where the two rates diverge.

## Leave Kd at 0

The derivative still spikes. One closed-loop sample read −1.16 per second while Z
itself was 1.42. Kd would follow that.

## The gate

Transfer and episode failed the first closed-loop gate because the free-run sample
starts at 2.2 s and the table now runs until about 3 s. The window measured a probe
step. Z was already near the request. Both checks now wait 4.5 s, force full width,
then take the same one-second sample. Re-run: transfer z_mean 1.456, episode 1.468,
both gates pass. That commit is `ff4e97c` on this branch.

The second after the table often prints rate 0. The pulse counter is still moving.
The rate EMA was cleared when the table finished. It is not a dead tachometer.
